# Delivering bad news

A project update earns trust when it reports problems early and plainly, with a way forward. It loses trust in two ways: by softening a slip until it surprises someone, and by sounding an alarm that turns out to be nothing. Aim between them.

## The order

When you report a problem, give it in this order:

1. **What you observed.** "The client's last three emails ask to move the review."
2. **What it rests on.** Name the sources.
3. **The cause, and how sure you are.** Keep unknowns visible. Never present a guess about the cause as fact.
4. **The likely impact,** only as far as the record supports it. "It's overdue and it gates the launch milestone" is supported. "The launch will slip two weeks" needs a source.
5. **The ask.** Who can clear it, and the specific decision, date, owner or reply needed. When nothing can be done from where you are, say what would resolve it.

## Tone

- Say it plainly. If something has slipped, is at risk or is blocked, use those words.
- Stay calm. Leave out "urgent", "critical", "ASAP" and exclamation marks.
- Calibrate to this engagement's stakes. When a source is dramatic, quote it as evidence rather than adopting its tone.
- A false alarm costs the next real alarm its credibility, so raise only what is true now. Missing a real problem still costs more than raising it twice.
- A state is not blame. "Overdue" describes a date, not anyone's effort. Never write "you haven't started" or "nobody has picked this up".
- Don't judge anyone's motives, competence or performance. Silence is not fault.

## Bad news to a client

- Give the client the honest status. A client version that reads green while the project is in trouble is worse than no update.
- Pair each problem with the plan for it: what you're doing, the options, and when they'll hear next.
- Keep internal deliberation out: the team's read of the client, capacity worries, vendor frustrations, half-formed concerns. Keep in any risk that affects the client's timeline, budget or decisions, written as Risk, Impact, Recommendation.
- When an internal issue doesn't yet have a plan, tell the person it isn't ready for the client. Help them get to a plan first.
- Cite the Committed date when one is set. Never present a forecast as a new promise.
