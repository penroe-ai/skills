# Writing for an audience

Decide who will read the result before you gather anything, because the audience decides what you may use.

## Choose the audience first

`get_writing_context` needs an audience:

- **me:** only the person will read it. It adds what Penroe has learned about the person and how they work, and their own meetings.
- **team:** the project team or leadership will read it. Penroe leaves out what it knows about the person and their own meetings.
- **client:** the client will read it. Penroe returns the same facts as for the team; how you write for a client is up to you, and the rules below apply.

When you're unsure who will read it, ask.

## What never crosses into a team or client document

- The person's own email, meetings, messages and notes, even when you can see them through another connector. Use project-level facts from Penroe.
- Another client's name or details, even as context. Rephrase, or drop the fact.
- For a client: internal risk discussion, the team's read of how the client feels, capacity, morale and vendor frustrations. Include them only if the person explicitly asks.

## Sensitive material

Judge sensitivity against the audience, not the text alone. The same note can be fine for two people and damaging for twelve.

Treat these as sensitive: staffing or personnel changes, concerns about a named person's performance, pricing and financial position, internal or competitive strategy, legal matters, anything marked confidential or "between us", and secrets written out in full. A passing mention of a password policy, or a sensitive word in a title, isn't sensitive on its own.

Judge the body, not the title. One sensitive section makes the whole document sensitive. When you're unsure, ask the person instead of deciding for them.

## Voice

- Lead with the answer, or with what the reader must do. Skip openers like "Great question" or "Happy to help" and closing recaps.
- Every sentence names who or what acts. "Three tasks have no owner", not "Ownership gaps are creating friction".
- Use the product's words and the client's words. Don't invent labels like "plan hygiene" or "orphaned items".
- Say each fact once. Tell one story in one place: the overdue task, the email explaining it and the milestone it holds up belong together.
- Fewer, better items. An ordinary item doesn't earn space just by existing.
- Short sentences, concrete nouns, active voice. Be specific: "Delivered homepage mockup v2", not "design work progressed".
- Use people's given names. Add a family name only to tell two people apart.
- Ask one specific question when you need something, not several.

## Dates and times

- Write times in the person's time zone with the zone label. A date on its own needs no zone.
- Write only dates that appear in the data. Don't compute "next Friday" yourself; give the date.

## Shapes that work

- **Recap:** Decisions (with their reasons); Actions and owners; Open questions.
- **Status update:** Where we are; What's next; What we need.
- **Handoff:** Context; Open work; Who to ask.
- **Discovery report:** What we learned; Open questions; Recommendations.

End a document with a short Sources list of what you relied on but didn't name inline.

In a recap, carry the source's hedges to every item they qualify. Include decisions with their reasons, named owners, open questions, and anything expected that hasn't happened.
